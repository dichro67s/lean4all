In a local ring the Jacobson radical of $(0)$ is the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$. So Mathlib's
general Nakayama lemma for ideals contained in the Jacobson radical applies to the finitely
generated module $M$:
(a) $M \subseteq \mathfrak{m}M$ gives $M = 0$ (`Submodule.eq_bot_of_le_smul_of_le_jacobson_bot`),
and $\top = \bot$ as submodules means `Subsingleton M`.
(b) $M \subseteq N + \mathfrak{m}M$ gives $M \subseteq N$
(`Submodule.le_of_le_smul_of_le_jacobson_bot`), which is the book's argument via $M/N$.
